Two steps, and they answer different questions

Finding a candidate and judging a candidate are separate problems, so they are separate actions. The scan produces a shortlist and stops there. Nothing is called a buy until you ask about one company specifically.

Step 01

Scan a sector

Choose a sector to find stocks receiving unusual recent social attention. Results are labelled Bullish, Bearish or Neutral — nothing stronger.

Step 02

Pick one ticker

Review the shortlist with price context, sentiment and how much evidence sits behind each row. No recommendation appears at this stage.

Step 03

Analyse it

Deep Analyze returns Buy, Watch or Avoid for that one ticker, with confidence, key reasons, risks, and the evidence the call rests on.

It will tell you when it cannot tell you anything

Most tools always produce a confident-looking answer, because a confident answer is what gets clicked. This one has an evidence floor. A ticker seen once is labelled Single mention and a ticker seen twice Limited signal — neither receives a sentiment reading at all. Only three or more scored posts earn Bullish, Bearish or Neutral.

The same restraint applies to sectors. On 19 August the tool marked healthcare as a single-name event rather than a sector move: one company accounted for 65% of that day’s increase in trading volume, at 124 times its own normal level. Knowing that is more useful than a green arrow over the whole sector.
